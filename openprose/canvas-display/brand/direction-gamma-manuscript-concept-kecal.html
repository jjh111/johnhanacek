Chapter IV · The OpenProse Method

On the writing of contracts, and how cadence keeps them.

A short essay on what happens when a piece of operating work is set down in plain language and signed by the hand that wrote it.

If a program is a document, and a document is a thing that can be set down in ink and held in the hand, then to declare an outcome is no more than to write the document with care, and to trust the work, as it runs on the cadence of the week, to keep the contract you have written.

iv.1 The file is fifteen lines. Anything longer is a system, not a contract.

Many operators have asked, in our time, what makes a program living and not dead. The answer, simply put, is that a program lives when it is held to a contract; and a contract lives when it can be inspected, refined, and signed by the same hand that wrote it.iv.1 The runtime keeps the contract, but the contract is yours to write. Without your hand on the file, the runtime has nothing to keep.

It is by this division of labor that the work of a Monday morning has changed. Where once an analyst opened seven dashboards and wrote a digest by hand, now the digest is declared in a file, and the runtime fetches the dashboards in their place. The analyst is freed to walk the buildings, to look at the boilers in their own place, and to write what they have seen.

§ iOf the writing of contracts.

A spec is no more than this: a thing required and a thing ensured. The thing required is the input the runtime must fetch; the thing ensured is the outcome the runtime must deliver. Between them, written in the same hand, lies the whole of the work.

Specimen weekly-energy-digest.prose · v42 requires:
  data_warehouse_url # meter records, 40 buildings
  codebase_ref

ensures:
  weekly growth, all claims quantified
  delivered Mondays at 09:00 ET

signed: sarah.k @ ridgeline · v42

Note that the spec does not specify the manner of the fetch, the wording of the digest, or the format of its delivery. These are the office of the runtime; the operator need only declare what must be true, and the runtime will make it so on the appointed cadence.

iv.2 A thing required, a thing ensured. The whole of the work is between them.

§ iiOf cadence, and the keeping.

Every Monday at the ninth hour, the runtime comes to the file as a scribe to the desk: it reads the contract anew, fetches the inputs, runs the work, and scores the outcome against the ensures-clauses. If the score falls below the contract by more than two parts in a hundred,iv.2 it holds delivery and pages the on-call.

It is not magic. It is not even cleverness. It is only the discipline of writing what you want, and the patience to refine the writing each time the runtime returns with a draft.

Of this discipline, we remember that, by the eighth week, the file had become sharper than the digest the analyst could have written by hand — the runtime having learned the operator's voice on the cadence of the operator's edits.

§ iiiWhy we call it a literary computer.

The spec is a thing of letters; the runtime is a thing of cadences; together they compose a document that improves itself at every passing. Hence we have written: a program is a document with guarantees, and have found no shorter words for what is true.

Here closes this chapter. The next, on errant specs and how to mend them, is set down elsewhere.
