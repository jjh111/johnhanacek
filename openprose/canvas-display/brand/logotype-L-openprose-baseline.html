II · Type Scale

Literata for prose. JetBrains Mono for facts.

Literata is the everything-type — display, body, pull-quotes, specimen samples. Italic does most of the emphasis work; bold is rare and reserved for metrics inside prose. JetBrains Mono handles code, program specifications, kickers, and small tabular figures (status, time, count).

V · Editorial Pairing

The voice, at length.

The openprose.ai voice is calm, specific, and unafraid of a short sentence. It names things by their nouns (requires, ensures, strategies), reaches for italic when italic is warranted, and reserves bold for metrics the reader needs to leave the paragraph with.

From the docs

Declarative contracts for the AI session.

A Prose program is a Markdown file with a contract — requires, ensures, strategies — not a chain of prompts. Forme auto-wires services by semantic match; any Prose Complete harness (Claude Code, Codex CLI, …) executes it.

The program declares what, not how, so it gets better as models improve. The lock-in is quality of operation, not proprietary infrastructure.

Your team doesn't prompt. Doesn't configure. Doesn't babysit. Outcomes arrive in your Slack, your email, or the systems you already use — on a schedule, with human review where it matters.

VII · Voice & Tone

Three sentences, one register.

Three sanctioned voices the brand uses: the short declaration, the spec-sheet note, the editorial observation. Each sentence can live on its own. None shout. All name the thing they're about.

Voice · declaration
“Offload the work you'd otherwise hire for.”

The headline. Seven words. Reads on a billboard, a business card, a homepage. Names the service in terms the P&L owner recognizes.

Voice · spec
“Flat monthly fee. Roughly $2,000 for two programs during pilot.”

Terms of trade spoken plainly. A clause that could sit in a contract. Carries warmth through cadence, not adjectives.

Voice · editorial
“ChatGPT and Claude are tools you use. OpenProse is a service you receive.”

The long-form voice. Names the thing, sets up a distinction, invites the reader to carry the distinction forward. Reads like an essay paragraph.
