II · Type Scale

Cinzel for the title. Cinzel for the brief. Cutive Mono for the keys.

A poster needs three voices. Cinzel at its heavier weights is the broadside title — inscriptional Roman shapes, all caps by default, the kind of letter cut into stone that reads from across a room. Cinzel at its lighter weights also carries the running brief — the same lapidary skeleton, just relaxed. Cutive Mono is the rare-monospace voice reserved for the spec keys, the version numbers, the formula symbols, the timestamp at the foot — a typewriter ribbon laid over the inscription. Italic violet is the writer's only emphasis; orange is held back for the trajectory line.

V · The Method Card

Every concept gets a numbered card, every card gets a small diagram.

ι borrows its defining gesture from the optimization-method poster: every concept the brand introduces gets its own numbered method card — a small numbered roundel, an all-caps title, a one-paragraph brief, and a tiny inline diagram. Concepts that need more space get more cards, never longer prose. The reader is invited to scan the cards; the body paragraphs above are there for those who want the connective tissue.

1
Declare the outcome
A responsibility is a .prose file with requires and ensures. Plain language, version-controlled, reviewable in any pull request.
2
Resolve the requires
The runtime fetches inputs — APIs, files, references — binds them, and verifies the binding before any work runs. The contract starts here.
3
Run on cadence
Every Monday at 09:00, the runtime executes the program. Eval scores are recorded. The trajectory line you see in the central figure is the page of every run plotted over time.
4
Verify against contract
Each result is scored against the ensures block. If the score falls below threshold, the runtime declines to deliver and surfaces the gap. No silent failures.

A method card is the brand's atomic unit of explanation. Six cards explain a layer; twelve cards explain the entire system. Cards never carry the wordmark, never carry the trajectory orange except inside their diagrams. The numbered roundel is the only ornament.

VI · Editorial Pairing

Set in type — the brief at length, with cards alongside.

The Reference Card voice is calm, specific, and willing to be cut into numbered cards. It explains, gives context, then trusts the reader to scan the diagrams alongside. The body paragraphs are connective tissue between method cards; the cards carry the load.

From the field notes

On the quiet arrival of Monday reports.

Once, the week began with a scramble. Someone opened seven dashboards, pulled the numbers, wrote the digest, and dropped it in the 09:00 standup. The rotation was a line in the budget. The standup was a line in the calendar. Nobody was quite sure what either accomplished beyond confirming that the dashboards were fine.

Then the outcome was declared in a file. Fifteen lines. Every Monday at 09:00 ET, the digest arrives — verified, signed, archived. The standup happens anyway, but it is fifteen minutes shorter. Nobody asks where the numbers came from. They are already in the document.

The first thing we noticed was what we stopped doing — the standing meetings where we'd review the dashboard to confirm the dashboard was fine.

The humanist computer is the insight that these two dispositions — craft and machinery — are not opposed. The machine handles the cadence. The prose handles the voice. The page reads the way a good analyst would have written it. The result is the point.

Maya Chen · Head of Operations · Series-C payments platform

VIII · Voice & Tone

Three sentences, one register, three placements.

Three sanctioned voices the brand uses: the short declaration, the spec-sheet note, the editorial observation. Each sentence can live on its own without ornament — and each demonstrates one placement of italic violet.

Voice · short · yellow
“Write the outcome. We run it.”

The tagline. Six words. The highlighter lands on the second sentence — the part that shifts the burden from the reader to the service.

Voice · spec · hot
“Billed by delivery. If the outcome doesn't ship, no line.”

Terms of art spoken plainly. Hot mark on the one word that defines the pricing model — the claim a reader cannot skim past.

Voice · editorial · italic
“Once, the week began with a scramble. Then the outcome was declared in a file.”

The long form voice. Italic carries the pivot. No highlighter here — this sentence is still the author's, not yet the reader's.
