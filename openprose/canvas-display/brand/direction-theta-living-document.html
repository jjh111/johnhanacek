I · Palette

Cocoa, cream, and a single ember of warmth.

II · Type Scale

Cormorant italic is the human intent. JetBrains Mono is the machine contract.

Two faces, two semantic layers. Cormorant Garamond italic carries every breath of human meaning — the wordmark, the manifesto sentence, the running paragraphs, the pull-quotes. JetBrains Mono carries the contract — the requires, the ensures, the eval scores, the run numbers, the timestamps. The two are not styled the same; they are not _meant_ to be styled the same. Italic prose says what the operator wants. Mono says what the runtime will guarantee. Read together they make a sentence that runs.

V · The Living Page

Italic is the human intent. The spec block is the machine contract. The page is the running program.

θ's defining mechanic isn't a mark on a phrase — it's that the page itself is the artifact. A .prose file isn't a code sample tucked into a section; it sits directly under the manifesto sentence. An ensures clause sharpens its wording in front of the reader, the way a good editor would refine a draft on a second pass. The eval score climbs because the program ran again. Time enters the page as accumulating content, not as animation flair. There is no separate "demo." The page is the demo.

// state · refining
The third ensures clause begins as delivered Mondays at 09:00 ET and, after eight seconds, sharpens to delivered Mondays @ 09:00 ET · verified · signed. The reader sees the system add to its own contract.
// state · climbing
The score in the run-state line ticks from 0.94 upward by 0.01 every twelve seconds. The run number ticks with it. Nothing else changes — but a page that climbs is alive in a way that a page that stays still is not.
// state · signed
The colophon at the foot is a real run-log: generated_at: 2026-05-04T13:18Z, signed: openprose-runtime-v0.42. The page declares its own provenance. The footer is the proof.
Rule · the spec The requires/ensures block lives directly under the manifesto sentence, not in a separate "code sample" panel. They are the same page; treating them otherwise is the brand's first lie.
Rule · the refinement Exactly one ensures clause refines itself per page. More than one becomes flair; zero becomes a screenshot. The single refinement is what makes the page legible as a draft being improved.
Rule · the climb Eval scores only ever climb — never reset to a number worse than the page started at. The brand promises improvement; the page must keep that promise even at idle.
Rule · reduced motion All three mechanics — refinement, climb, and sparkline — respect prefers-reduced-motion: reduce. The page stays alive in spirit; it just doesn't move when the reader has asked it not to.
VI · Editorial Pairing

Set in type — the voice at length, with the spec running through it.

The Living Document voice is calm, specific, and unafraid of an inline spec annotation. It names things by their nouns. It reaches for italic only when italic carries a pivot. The whole page reads as if the system itself dictated this paragraph after running the brief one more time.

# field-note · run 41 · score 0.93

On the quiet arrival of Monday reports.

Once, the week began with a scramble. Someone opened seven dashboards, pulled the numbers, wrote the digest, and dropped it in the 09:00 standup. The rotation was a line in the budget cost: 40 op-hours/quarter. The standup was a line in the calendar. Nobody was quite sure what either accomplished beyond confirming that the dashboards were fine.

Then the outcome was declared in a file. Fifteen lines. Every Monday at 09:00 ET, the digest arrives — verified · signed · archived. The standup happens anyway, but it is fifteen minutes shorter. Nobody asks where the numbers came from. They are already in the document.

The first thing we noticed was what we stopped doing — the standing meetings where we'd review the dashboard to confirm the dashboard was fine.

The literary computer is the insight that these two dispositions — craft and machinery — are not opposed. The machine handles the cadence cadence: weekly. The prose handles the voice tone: executive-friendly. The page reads the way a good analyst would have written it. The result is the point.

Maya Chen · Head of Operations · signed: maya@ridgeline.example
