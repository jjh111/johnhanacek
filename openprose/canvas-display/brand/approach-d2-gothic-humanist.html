I.   The Concept
1

Responsibilities
as a service.

You have responsibilities you want to offload. Today your options are: hire someone, or buy a tool and learn to use it. Both cost you attention. The former in management, the latter in mastery.

OpenProse is a third option. You describe the responsibilities: what needs to happen, what good looks like, what the guardrails are. It handles them. No dashboard to check. No tool to learn. Results show up in Slack or email on a cadence, like a good employee who checks in when they have something to report.

We call this a self-maintaining book of responsibilities. You write what needs to happen. The book keeps itself up.

II.   vs Using Claude Directly
2

Claude does what you tell it.
OpenProse does what you need.

You can use Claude to do this yourself. But then you're the manager: prescribing tasks, maintaining context, iterating on prompts. Your mental overhead never goes away.

We're the manager. You can use Claude directly, but then you're the one managing. We're not selling a tool. We're offloading a responsibility.

Every agent framework today is imperative: prescribe tasks, manage execution. OpenProse is declarative. Describe the outcome, set the contract, let it run. The difference between constant oversight and setting the goal, then getting out of the way.

Declarative approaches (SQL, Terraform, React) have consistently won in domains where "what" matters more than "how." Agent orchestration is the next domain. OpenProse is the first declarative agent programming language.
