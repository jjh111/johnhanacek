The Language

Responsibilities
as code.

You have responsibilities you want to offload. Today your options are: hire someone, or buy a tool and learn to use it. Both cost you attention.

OpenProse is a third option. Write .prose files that describe what needs to happen, what good looks like, what the guardrails are. The runtime handles the rest.

Declarative. Like SQL. Like Terraform. Like React.
"What" not "how."

weekly-report.prose
# A responsibility, not a prompt responsibility "Weekly Revenue Report" { schedule: "every Monday 8am" data: @stripe + @postgres outcome: "Executive summary with week-over-week trends, anomaly flags, and recommended actions" quality: { accuracy: "numbers match Stripe" tone: "direct, no filler" length: "under 500 words" } deliver: #finance-channel guardrails: [ "never share raw customer data" "flag anomalies over 15% delta" ] }
Architecture

Three layers.
One stack.

01 Layer One

Language

The .prose file format. Human-readable declarations that describe responsibilities, outcomes, quality contracts, and delivery schedules. Not prompts. Not scripts. Specifications.

02 Layer Two

Framework

The orchestration layer. Parses .prose files, plans multi-agent workflows, manages retries, quality checks, and escalation paths. You never touch this.

03 Layer Three

Runtime

Execution engine. Connects to your data sources, manages agent pools, handles scheduling, and delivers results to the channels you specify. Self-healing. Self-maintaining.

Workflow

From zero to
running responsibility

01

Describe It

Write a .prose file. Define the responsibility: what outcome you need, how often, where it delivers, and what quality looks like.

02

Connect It

Point to your data sources. Stripe, Postgres, Salesforce, internal APIs. The framework handles authentication and access patterns. Read-only to start.

03

Forget It

Results arrive on schedule in Slack, email, or wherever you specified. Like a good senior employee who checks in when they have something to report. No dashboard. No overhead.
