I · Palette

Ivory and sepia, with a warm rust accent and two semantic signals: green for live, red for held.

A living palette. Ivory #f7f1e3 carries the page; the same warm cream every classical type system has used. Sepia ink #221408 reads soft on ivory, never harsh. Rust #a8552a is the editorial accent — the italic, the section kicker, the link, the second voice in the automaton. Green #1f8a4c is the positive signal — live, delivered, OK; red #c0392b is the negative signal — held, failed, warn. Rust never carries status; it stays the writer's voice. The whole palette feels like watching a system maintain itself: warm reliable sepia with a clear two-tone signal layer for state.

V · The Living Margins

Italic rust is the writer. The pill is the reader. The margins are alive.

η's defining gesture is the cellular automaton crawling the left and right margins of every page — Rule 110, sixteen cells per side, advancing one row every fourteen frames in rust and amber. The brand demonstrates itself by growing. Italic rust is the writer's emphasis, same role italic-red plays in α. Three pills carry status & rhythm: green for the good news (delivered, live, OK), red reserved for the single attention claim per page (held, missed, failed), and the bare rust pill as the reader's pause — soft, opt-in, no status meaning. Green and red carry weight; rust just slows the eye.

Rule · green = good Use on phrases that name a positive system state: delivered, verified, live, OK, shipped. Green is the brand saying "this is the good news."
Rule · red = attention Reserve for the single fail-closed claim per page — held, missed, doesn't ship, failed. Red is only for the moment the reader must look up.
Rule · rust = rhythm The bare rust pill is reader's pause, never status. Use on the nouns and numbers a busy operator would scroll back to — a soft wash, no positive or negative meaning. Never on verbs.
Rule · the automaton The Rule-110 cellular automaton is a page-level ornament, never embedded inside content. It crawls the left and right margins as the reader scrolls; it never crosses into the content column. Pause it on prefers-reduced-motion.
Rule · the mark Never on the logotype. Never on a button. Pills and the automaton are prose tools only — body copy, editorial H2/H3, the page-bg layer. Not chrome.
VI · Editorial Pairing

Set in type — the voice at length, with the margins still growing.

The organism voice is calm, specific, and unafraid of a short sentence. It names things by their nouns. It reaches for italic teal only when italic is warranted. The page reads as if the system itself is keeping you company — the margins growing slowly while you read.

From the field notes

On the quiet arrival of Monday reports.

Once, the week began with a scramble. Someone opened seven dashboards, pulled the numbers, wrote the digest, and dropped it in the 09:00 standup. The rotation was a line in the budget. The standup was a line in the calendar. Nobody was quite sure what either accomplished beyond confirming that the dashboards were fine.

Then the outcome was declared in a file. Fifteen lines. Every Monday at 09:00 ET, the digest arrives — verified, signed, archived. The standup happens anyway, but it is fifteen minutes shorter. Nobody asks where the numbers came from. They are already in the document.

The first thing we noticed was what we stopped doing — the standing meetings where we'd review the dashboard to confirm the dashboard was fine.

The humanist computer is the insight that these two dispositions — craft and machinery — are not opposed. The machine handles the cadence. The prose handles the voice. The page reads the way a good analyst would have written it. The result is the point.

Maya Chen · Head of Operations · Series-C payments platform

VIII · Voice & Tone

Three sentences, one register, three marks.

Three sanctioned voices the brand uses: the short declaration, the spec-sheet note, the editorial observation. Each sentence can live on its own without ornament — and each demonstrates one placement of the teal pill.

Voice · short · teal pill
“Write the outcome. We run it.”

The tagline. Six words. The pill lands on the second sentence — the part that shifts the burden from the operator to the system that maintains itself.

Voice · spec · amber pill
“Billed by delivery. If the outcome doesn't ship, no line.”

Terms of art spoken plainly. Amber pill on the one word that defines the pricing model — the warm complement carrying the boundary that the cool teal accent cannot.

Voice · editorial · italic
“Once, the week began with a scramble. Then the outcome was declared in a file.”

The long form voice. Italic teal carries the pivot. No pill here — this sentence is still the author's, not yet the system's. The automaton keeps growing in the margins regardless.
