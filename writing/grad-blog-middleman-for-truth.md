# [A Middleman for Truth](https://blogs.commons.georgetown.edu/cctp-820-fall2015/a-middleman-for-truth/)

Reports of the death of the web have been greatly exaggerated. Reports of the growth of monopoly power in Internet services have not.

The app-ification of the web has elicited pixels and ink from commentators, but still web standards remain useful. Google itself is planning to merge Android and ChromeOS. Having a cyberspace that can be accessed by any browser with no need to install software locally is powerful and will remain so. A company I consult for uses webRTC for its video chat implementation, which means no installation of plugins or other little programs to the local machine, just the usage of standards in browser.

With the advent of “deep linking” even apps are gaining some of the hyperlinking functionality of the web. From my perspective I see the web and local apps rubbing off on each other, which will lead to a gradual evolutionary reconceptualization of what the Internet is to users.

This reconceptualization stands at a crossroads though, as commenters like Zittran discuss. There is a war going on between open standards and proprietary platforms.

Google becoming a primary gateway to the Internet carries immense implications.

Google allows a huge amount of cognitive offloading, which has been studied. Now networked humans do not need to remember information, instead we can remember much smaller pointers to information. Rather than recall the encyclopedia, we only need to enter a search string. This allows any one person to know a lot more. Yet Google is not forwarding truth, it is merely returning results that correlate to search strings. If you search for peer-reviewed science, you’ll find it. If you search for “vaccines cause autism” you will find some garbage science.

Either way your string is dealt with and you are returned results. It is up to the individual doing the query to ascertain the validity of the information. Google is not in the truth-making business.

Or is it?

Already Jenny McCarthy feels confident enough in the list of links returned from the first page of a (leading) Google search to claim that she got a degree from the “University of Google”. This is of course ridiculous, but Google has no incentive to debase the validity of its own results. So it does nothing to stop ignorant people from worshipping it as an Answer Engine. Indeed Google continues to forward the idea that results on its site are helpful – it even implies that they are true. It wants to make a “knowledge” graph, “knowledge” implies truth. Google has a big incentive to become your go-to source for answers to questions – look no further than their recent ad campaign encouraging you to Google various strings.

As we have discussed in this class, Google is not aiming to remain a mere search engine that returns links, but rather aims to transcend into a kind of Answer Engine. I have written about this trajectory before. With improvements in string-handling techniques combined with mergers with software already available to generate narrative stories from data we will find one day that a Google search returns a list of links and an automatically generated summary of the top N number of links’ content.

[This could further improve the ability of individuals to become functional cognitive collectives, as they will be further empowered to work with wider accumulated knowledge and increasingly real-time data.](http://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/futuresource/internet-as-answer-engine-part-i)And [It could also open them up to manipulation, for the “answer” that an Answer engine delivers will not necessarily be true, it will simply be.](http://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/futuresource/internet-as-answer-engine-part-ii)

It could inform, or it could reinforce ignorance, or it could even be just the morally skewed practice of advertising trying to twist you into thinking that a product can solve your search string, regardless of whether it can truly.

It comes down to trust but verity verify. Trusting systems is a heuristic, a way to reduce complexity — like trusting that the card system in the library will get you to the desired book. Yet If you trust a system blindly you have lost your agency. So go to page 2 of the results. Run the query again with a modified string. Be academic about it. You are a minority in the world: you know how to research. It is your duty to help those you care about to stay above trust; teach them to verify.

To me this is the ultimate point of this class: design thinking is about learning to see through bullshit and stay above manipulation. Your attention is worth money. Your rational thought is not prioritized in a consumer economy. So prioritize logic and empower yourself; no company will do that for you.
