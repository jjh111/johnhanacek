# [Where Does Meaning Reside? – more questions than answers](https://blogs.commons.georgetown.edu/cctp-820-fall2015/where-does-meaning-reside-more-questions-than-answers/)

I have just started flipping around in Hofstadter’s Godel, Escher, Bach to explore it a bit, and I encountered the concept of isomorphism: that meaning arises when there is an isomorphism between rule-governed symbols and the real world. What caught my interest most keenly is Hofstadter briefly discussing how people can make mistakes in understanding when inside language and attribute meaning to the objects, the words themselves, rather than the link between the object and the world.

This is similar to what is going on in quantum computing, where the information is stored in the interrelationships between the qubits, not in the actual qubits themselves.

And this is similar to the debate we had last night about “technology will bring us together” and trying to separate out the meaning of “technology” and “us” and “together”, which was really enjoyable – wait, what is fun then??? oh never mind, we’ll leave that one.

So where is meaning made? By linking the symbol and the world?

Meaning is not carried in the message, but rather is created by the processing of the information of the message. Information is the “thing” that fills the space between connections, information rides collections of temporally encoded matter and when it hits an information processor it has the chance to become meaningful. Is there a gradient to meaning? Some kind of hierarchy? Is a computer processing information into a video doing any kind of meaning making? Or can only humans make meaning? Perhaps there are many nested levels of meaning, or it is a continuum…

It seems also to depend on what level of observation one is located. Descriptions of systems can be extremely different depending on the scale one is considering. Seldom do we humans try to hold all the descriptions of our body system–from DNA to organs to psychological swirlings–and when we do it creates very poor theories. The human body system has so many levels that it becomes a blur quickly: all levels seem to be correlated to each other, and each level is made of correlations.

Is this nested set of levels part of how consciousness functions? In order to make meaning, do we need to sift information through many sets of levels? As it sifts does it become more symbolic? Is our mind working as a kind of compressor, taking in the world and throwing away the details save for the essences of what is needed to form a link in our minds to the more complex world? Like straining a particulate through ever finer sieves?

Or is it about adding blurriness? Are human minds and the cognition we know the result of very high abstractions above our “hardware” so that we can be blurry in language?

Are we “simplifying” the world into symbols, or are we “complicating” it into symbols?

Information is the link between communicating nodes, these can be arranged in a network. Except that the ideas of networks that we have today are very digital/binary tech oriented. We model networks as clouds of points connected by lines. We make network topologies.  
When we should actually make network topographies. There are probabilities inside the network of new connections being made, and there are probabilities of paths that information will most likely take, often dictated by connections, but also influenced by more subtle properties.

The “Information Revolution” is Conceptual. “Computing” is a matter of creativity, it’s a matter of devising ways to hold, manipulate and read back the sate of energy quanta contained in a system. So what was once current-based electrical computing employing billions of incidental electrons is now approaching the level of individual electron and photon counting.

Technology is harnessed phenomena, as Brian Arthur elegantly phrases it. Thus the first fire used to cook mammoth meat and the engine in a 747 are the same phenomena harnessed in different ways.

Computing is the same. Currently we are like cavemen, chanting around barely refined and scarcely harnessed digital computer fire. We have only just begun to dream of what other forms computing can take.

Currently we are still locked to the approach that each individual bit in a computer is discrete and isolated. Read and manipulated one at a time, 1s and 0s constitute the linear operation of the digital computer we know. Turing’s conception is the basis for all the digital computers we know.

This is not the only way to compute. The photon teaches us about the raw universal version of computation. By default, the universe does not isolate the influences of particles and energy. The photon when it is computing in a human system is a single value, or part of a single value that will be summed, multiplied, divided or what have you in logical operations. The photon when it is computing in a “universal” system is a waveform, maximally influencing perhaps a single point, but subtly changing the state of all around it.

Consider a glass of water. Now consider a waterfall. Your mind could imagine both. The universe contains both. We can make computer simulations of both. But the problem becomes one of fractal scale. Modern computers can simulate the surface features of the water, maybe even use a few macro “particles” at key convergence points to accurately model waves. Modern computers are not simulating water, only the surface.

Are our minds any different? Do we ever “hold” the meaning or does meaning only exist in the link? Are we just playing with surfaces when we imagine? Could computers not imagine too?
