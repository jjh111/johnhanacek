# [From the Cloud to the Atmosphere: the packetization of compute](https://blogs.commons.georgetown.edu/cctp-820-fall2015/from-the-cloud-to-the-atmosphere-the-packetization-of-compute/)

A new design direction for the Internet is emerging that would see the net transform from communications substrate to computing substrate. The future of Internet might very well go from a patchwork of different clouds to a unified compute substrate where the cloud providers peer with one another.

As all the things continue to get computerized it will prove increasingly impossible to move the data from censors to central clouds. The devices on the “edge of the network” will increasingly be tasked with processing and structuring data. They will turn to one another for context and to share computing power.

The future direction of an ambient computing substrate will require reconceptualization of what can be a “packet.” In the future, the Internet may grow an abstraction: the ability for virtual machines to exist within the ambient ether of the whole net.

The base-base layer will likely remain the TCP/IP packet (although UDP is growing in adoption due to its smoother streaming affordances, and we’ll want to watch how it meshes in with the wider Internet) but above the datagram layer there are new proposals to make higher level abstracted packet clusters.

What will these packet clusters be? How will they work? This depends on how the future of the blockchain goes. The blockchain represents a potential new design direction for the Internet where networks of computers function as a database made of modular elements (blocks) that are strung together on a chain one after the other. Each time an entry is made in a blockchain database every computer in the network races to be the first to secure the block into the chain. Crypotgraphic keys are generated that are unique to the peer who initiated the addition.

People have talked about blockchain for keeping track of a money, but it is more profound: imagine a network that is one unified database comprised of packets of virtual machines. Already the cloud architecture itself works like this: internally in the large datacenters the servers form a fabric where virtual machine instances are given internal addresses and function in a constellation where each machine can grow and shrink in “size” as needed.

Ethereum is an approach to use a blockchain to create virtual machines that are powered by the power in the Ethereum blockchain network. Ethereum has a paradigm of “distributed apps” or D-apps in their parlance. These are essentially ad-hoc virtual machines that are powered by the aggregate computing power in the Ethereum network. This is profound since it is a transition from “this web app lives in X location” to “this web app is capable of running in X network”. We are witnessing the furthering of the end for the idea of a dedicated 1:1 mapping of place to compute.

The blockchain is a design methodology that shows us a pathway to make the wider Internet into such a malleable compute fabric. The cloud architecture paradigm of today can be pushed out into a global Internet. There will still be giant datacenters, but they could be merged into the ether rather than living on their own. The Internet will continue on an abstraction trajectory – moving from communications network meshing hardware addresses to cyberspace meshing software addresses.

  

D-App schematic

The future will see an ambient ether where finding the “owner” or “location” of applications could become much more challenging. A D-app version of Silk Road or Megaupload would have no single place to shut it down. It would resist the ability for a network “owner” to exist, since it could be configured where every single peer has their own key to a part of the database, and no global keys exist.

How governments will respond to the evolution of the Internet from connection to computation will be constant struggle. Will the net stay wild or will it become a tool for power? Somewhere in the middle the answer lies, and the dynamics of all the actors will be challenging. We are still only in the proto-beginnings of where the Internet can go. From communication to computing the Internet is evolving, and concepts are merging. Cyberspace will become an increasingly apt metaphor.

This brings us around to this class and the systems thinking curriculum: it will become ever more needed. As computing becomes more pervasive it is also set to become more “magical,” harder to grasp yet more and more powerful through the abstraction. The next generations are being born into a world where APIs call APIs call APIs, where abstraction levels are between each other and the “bottom” of the computing ether becomes extremely difficult to find. We will need to train future generations to be structure archaeologists. They will need to remember that whenever computing is concerned there is a “bottom”. We will need to foster a whole new discipline of Design Justice that asks humanist questions about the myriad of convergent technologies.

Technology is becoming too advanced for the technologists – technology like art is a reflection of humanity, and thus humanity must be involved in its creation and management. The world we are heading toward could feature technology building lords ruling over user peasants, or it could feature a world of peers working together to negotiate justice. Part of the challenge will be visualizing and discussing complexity. This Leading By Design curriculum is what children should learn in school – I call this class “Defense against Dark Information” and that is a curriculum that every child deserves. The world needs cogent systems thinkers, and it is part of our mission to spread the knowledge of how to see the world as made of inter-dependencies.

The next cultural revolution will be in thinking not just about things, but also about the spaces between the things – the next cultural revolution will be when the citizens of Earth learn to think in dynamical systems paradigms. Everyone deserves to understand how to see.
