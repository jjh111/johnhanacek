# [Build as Evolution Does, Not As Humans Do](https://blogs.commons.georgetown.edu/cctp-820-fall2015/build-as-evolution-does-not-as-humans-do/)

The iPhone is Actually Quite Simplistic.

As the Simon article describes how humans problem solve, we can see that the iPhone is a reproduction of a complex system design. Its conceptual DNA is not much different than the laptop of 2003, or the desktop of 1998. It has added new organs and shrunk in size, which change its contextual interactions with the sociotechnical environment. However the iPhone is a simplistic device. It has not added any hierarchical complexity since its “bottom” is still the principle of adding and erasing bits.

Moreover, the iPhone is a design that wishes it was just a mathematical artifact, rather than fully embracing that it is a physical one. It is a massive compromise game where processors designed for room-scale computing are crammed into a tiny rectangle. The iPhone is based on processors that were never meant for its form factor. We must re-conceptualize the physical methods to achieve computing. The iPhone gets hot as it processes information, your hand gets hot as it metabolizes glucose into ATP – currently the iPhone and your hand are fighting each other to stay cool while both being hot. In the future this can change, in the future your hand and your iPhone can work together – your hand heat can power your iPhone.

Currently we build many of our technologies from the level of imagination, then instantiate them in reality. I argue that we should create a feedback loop: imagine products, but also learn hard science so that your imagination is informed with the deeper affordances of the physical universe. Because there are deeper affordances in the universe than our symbolic thought systems currently understand.

I want to get us out of awe for our artifacts and into deep consideration of the affordances of the physical universe. What we build today is often the result of us trying to make the physical universe fit our imagination. Rather than letting the physical universe inspire our imagination. For example: the way we build modern processors is really foolish if we learn a few things about thermodynamics.

As Simon describes, equilibration is a feature of physical systems. If we partition clumps of atoms into walls separating rooms and set each room a different temperature (energy state) we will find that, over time, the rooms will gradually equalize their energy state.

The modern digital computer, such as the iPhone, is such a room in the context of its environment. It is a space insulated from the external energy state of its environment. The computer processor is a space that has been further subdivided into individual “rooms” that can pump in or out charge state. Whenever this happens though there is a thermodynamic cost. Compressing a physical system that has two existing variables into one state expression means that the overall energy of the system must be retained. However, the size of the transistor rooms did not get any “larger” to accommodate the influx of energy now put into it. So it releases as heat.

  
However, this principle works backwards. Instead of obsessing about the literal instructions of a mathematical computer, we could re-connect the physicality of the operation of “computing” and begin to build transistor arrays that would absorb heat. So a few transistors could be dedicated to capturing the heat made by the mathematical bit erasures. We could build systems which are much more self contained: rather than just radiating heat outward a future processor could store energy in itself and use its own energy to compute. Rather than drawing on a large battery and losing energy as heat, it could draw on a smaller battery and store its heat in bins and then re-insert that heat as computation in adjacent bins.

Anyway, ok the point here is that as long as we focus on human symbolic cognition as our design space we will create sub-optimal systems. We need to focus on what we have learned with human symbolic cognition, mainly the principles of thermodynamics. This will allow us to create computer systems that do not just pump in energy and output HEAT+computing, but instead allow us to build system that pump in energy and output computing with minimal waste heat. Hold onto the heat. Use the reversibility of physical interactions to our advantage. Learn physics and build from that level up instead of trying to make physics fit our symbolic desires.
