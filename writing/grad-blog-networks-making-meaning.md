# [Networks Making Meaning in Networks: the future of direction of “computing”](https://blogs.commons.georgetown.edu/cctp-820-fall2015/networks-making-meaning-in-networks-the-future-of-computing/)

Modern digital personal computers are little language boxes, they are devices that follow scripted grammars of action to manipulate energy states of a physical system. The human mind is similar in definition, yet it can invent new grammars, it is the programmer of itself and the artifacts of its creation, and the computer is the recipient of that program. People also program one another. The human mind as a “processor” is gaining credence as we learn that the mind is born relatively blank and is then filled with surfaces and essences, filled with meaning made from experience as the internal chemical state and neural network of the brain configures itself.  
Yet in the human mind, processing leads to things we can call ontologically “new” despite being recombinations of previous experiences. The digital computers struggle to do this. For a computer, “garbage in/garbage out” has been the mantra. For a human brain, garbage in does not always mean such a literal repetition. Humans are incredible at filtering out noise and finding patterns, then appropriating those patterns for later use in other life-experiences.

What is it about the human that is fundamentally different than the digital computer? It’s simple: The traditional digital computer is not based on distributed paradigms. The computer is at best a relational comparison machine, it can look at any state of stored sign vehicles instantiated in it and compare them with other sign vehicles instantiated in it.

The human mind is processing using interrelation networks, not discrete variables read linearly in/out.

The revolution in thought that got us to digital computers was the idea that seemingly analog events and concepts could be digitized, counted and broken into tiny conceptual blocks for manipulation by mechanical means. This means a focus on manipulating the “sign vehicle” element of symbolic cognition. So the units of manipulation are 0 and 1, on or off. These are vehicles since they relate symbolic states to physical states. Just as an A written here and an A written on the board and an A said aloud are all the sign vehicles, so is an A stored in bits on digital memory systems. A single ‘A’ or a single ‘0’ in between a sender and a receiver, or even ferried around a complex network of transceivers, is information in the middle and meaning at the interface. That is, the sign vehicle carries the information from point to point in the network, while the transceiver makes meaning of it. So humans do this with our experience, culture, etc; while computers do this with pre-defined grammars, such as codecs and other standards. Humans are making meaning with a flexible, adaptable library, while computers are not.

Why? Because the human transceiver is a network itself. For meaning’s construction, we need fractal networks. As Prof. Irvine’s paper describes, the symbolic faculty forms a web and continuum of symbolic cognition and meaning making. (Irvine, 7) So that individuals and culture are all woven together in meaning making. The Internet has been showing us the power of this as memes and new syntactical forms ripple through the conversational atmosphere of the Internet. In the Internet information is in the middle between transcievers, and this allows new meaning to be made even though the constituent parts of the Internet involve many “dumb” sign-routers.

The next “computing” revolution upon us is the quantization of inter-relationship properties of systems. The next computers are not using the discrete manipulation of the sign vehicles, but rather they are concerned with the inter-relational network itself as their unit of manipulation. So we see this in “neural networks” used in deep learning on digital computers, and in quantum computer qubit lattice configurations. In each case we find similar paradigm: rather than concern oneself with the specific manipulation of discrete signs, instead create a platform where the system is inter-related to itself enough so that activity in one area ripples through to another area and changes the overall state in subtle ways. Then start it in one way and have it move through time and use the resultant shift in the network’s overall state to function as the “answer”.

This is much more how the human brain functions. The human brain is an interconnected three dimensional correlated electrochemical scale-free network. So neurons that fire together wire together, in the simplest terms. The neurons are electrical, but work in action potentials so that their voltage amplitudes are modulated by the influx of ions: more ions more voltage. This is a relatively analog system in that the action potential propagates as a wave down the axon. Yet it does become quantized at points. In between neurons are gap junctions where axon tails meet dendrites of the next neuron. Here the ionic electrical information carried in the action potential is converted back into chemical units, neurotransmitters, and these are then sent across the divide. These are the sign vehicles of human inter-cognitive thought.

Information occupies the space between each transcieving element, whether it is neurons in brains or humans in societies.

Information occupies the space in the middle in human cultural-symbolic-technological systems. In artifacts such as computers, information is in the artifact. Then by the human interfacing with the artifact can that human make meaning, as now the information creates a connection and fills that connective space. Then the human at the interface can make meaning by the information correlating elements of their neural network.

Meaning-making is a property of networked interrelation and transcieving elements. Information is in the middle and meaning is made at the interfaces between the middle. With sufficient density and interrelation, any network can begin to repurpose experience into proto-meaning, and as the endeavor continues move toward genuine insight.

Once computers are able to place information in the middle between their components (within themselves) then they will be able to create meaning. There is nothing magical about meaning-making. We have seen from Pinker and Irvine’s accounts that language evolved. It is not unique to humans by physical law rather we are simply the ones in whom it evolved. Our computers previously can be considered like animals in the wild: limited to pre-programmed grammars. The computers of tomorrow will be meaning-makers with us since they will be able to place information in the middle of themselves, like human brains can do. By transforming our computers from language boxes to language networks we will enable them to create their own meaning. What will they create? Difficult to say. Could you have asked neolithic humans to guess what their future kin would infer from the world?

Food for your thinking: if you feed a computer “neural network” back on itself enough times the network begins to invent forms that were not in the image. This system was trained to “see” using images of dogs, so it sees the world in dogs, but notice how this “sight” is not 1:1; the computer here is beginning to infer form.
